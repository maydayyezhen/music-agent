# Source Study: Percy Goetschius — Exercises in Melody-Writing

## Source identity

- Author: Percy Goetschius
- Title: *Exercises in Melody-Writing*
- Publication year: 1903
- Source type: public-domain music-theory / melody-writing treatise
- Study scope: complete readable main text, reviewed in three batches
- Main text conclusion in the studied scan: PDF p. 128
- Purpose in this repository: extract reusable melody-composition principles without importing source-specific example melodies or common-practice-era restrictions as universal law

This study is **source evidence**, not default composition memory.

Do not load it during ordinary composition unless the current task explicitly asks to revisit, verify or compare the source.

---

## Evidence discipline

The book is an early-20th-century tonal pedagogy text. It contains both:

1. broadly reusable structural ideas; and
2. historical style / exercise restrictions.

The study therefore separates:

```text
source observation
-> reusable invariant
-> V2 promotion decision
```

Exact example melodies, complete phrase transcriptions, full harmony and source-specific rhythmic sequences are intentionally not stored here.

---

# 1. High-level melody quality

## Direct source observation

The opening chapters organize good melodic conduct around three broad qualities:

- local coherency;
- unity of the complete melodic sentence;
- enough variety to avoid monotony.

## Reusable invariant

Melody quality is not reducible to scale membership or chord-tone membership.

Useful generic critic dimensions are:

```text
local coherence
structural unity
controlled variety
```

## Promotion decision

Promoted to `melody-structure-development` as high-level critic goals.

---

# 2. Basic melodic motion

## Direct source observation

The early course distinguishes smooth scale-line motion from more disjunct chord-line motion. Repeated tones are also treated as legitimate melodic material.

The text initially regulates skips tightly, especially wide skips, but later permits many exceptions when larger harmonic or recurrent structure explains them.

## Reusable invariant

A compact abstract motion vocabulary is useful:

```text
STEP
REPEAT
LEAP
TURN
ARRIVE
RESOLVE
```

Larger leaps should be evaluated in context rather than rejected by size alone.

## Historical restriction not promoted

The early training preference to reverse direction after a wide leap is not treated as a universal rule.

---

# 3. Context can justify local irregularity

## Direct source observation

Across several chapters, the book repeatedly allows motions that appear irregular in isolation when a larger explanation is present, including:

- a convincing chord-line;
- recurrent rhythmic design;
- exact or sequential recurrence;
- phrase / cadence structure;
- later modulation context.

## Reusable invariant

```text
recognizable larger structure
can legitimize local surprise
```

## Promotion decision

Promoted into the generic melody critic as an `explain before penalize` principle.

---

# 4. Rhythm is part of melodic syntax

## Direct source observation

The pedagogy begins with uniform rhythm, then introduces duration diversity, rests and irregular metric placement.

The text explicitly observes that rhythmic irregularity can become intelligible when it recurs at corresponding locations.

Rests can preserve or clarify melodic coherency and structural unity when they articulate meaningful boundaries.

## Reusable invariant

Pitch and rhythm should not be treated as fully independent streams.

Useful concepts:

```text
rhythmic-melodic group
metrical footprint
rest as phrase punctuation
recurrence support for syncopated / marked rhythm
```

## Historical restriction not promoted

The book's regular-versus-irregular metric hierarchy is not used to reject modern syncopation.

---

# 5. Phrase relationship: statement and response

## Direct source observation

The Period chapters introduce Antecedent / Consequent relations and gradually move from highly parallel construction toward increasingly contrasting construction.

The important mechanism is not the exact 4+4 measure textbook form. It is the controlled degree of correspondence between phrases.

## Reusable invariant

Phrase relation can be treated as a continuum:

```text
nearly exact
-> lightly modified
-> partially derived
-> strongly contrasting
```

## Promotion decision

Promoted as explicit phrase-relation planning and `development distance` reasoning.

## Historical restriction not promoted

Four-measure Phrase, eight-measure Period and classical cadence locations remain common-practice examples, not universal templates.

---

# 6. Melody syntax through recurrence

## Direct source observation

Chapter X explicitly moves beyond note-by-note succession and introduces recurrence of whole tone-groups through:

```text
Repetition
Sequence
```

The initial group may be short, measure-length, multi-measure or phrase-length.

The text then states that recurrence can justify otherwise irregular melodic succession.

## Reusable invariant

A melody generator needs explicit group / motif identity.

Later material should often be derived rather than regenerated from scratch.

## Promotion decision

Promoted to the generic Skill as:

```text
repeat
sequence
motif identity
structural recurrence
```

---

# 7. Modified recurrence

## Direct source observation

The following chapters allow recurrence to be modified through:

- adding intermediate tones;
- omitting material;
- widening or narrowing intervals;
- partial recurrence;
- rhythmic modification;
- changed rhythmic location;
- contrary direction;
- broad application to the Consequent phrase.

The source repeatedly emphasizes that modification should not destroy recognizability.

## Reusable invariant

A useful development engine should expose transformations such as:

```text
repeat
sequence
fragment
partial recurrence
interval modification
rhythmic variation
augmentation / diminution
contour inversion
```

The key quality test is:

```text
changed enough to develop
but recognizable enough to remain related
```

## Promotion decision

Promoted to the generic Skill as development operators, not mandatory APIs.

---

# 8. Chromaticism and modulation

## Direct source observation

The book distinguishes altered scale-steps from actual modulation and later expands from tightly restricted related-key modulation toward chromatic, overlapping-scale and sequence/cadence-based modulation.

Common tones and pauses can support reinterpretation into another key.

## Reusable invariant

A chromatic note should not be classified merely as `out_of_scale = error`.

Possible roles include:

```text
local color
connector
tension
harmonic reinterpretation
modulation marker
```

## Historical restrictions not promoted

Specific altered-scale-step tables, related-key priority and common-practice modulation procedures remain tonal-style knowledge rather than universal melody rules.

---

# 9. Essential versus embellishing tones

## Direct source observation

Division Two explicitly states that many melody tones are secondary adjuncts serving to connect or embellish more important tones.

Duration is only a rough clue. The deeper distinction depends strongly on harmonic / chord-line relation.

## Reusable invariant

```text
surface note count
!=
structural note count
```

Also:

```text
chord tone
!= automatically structural tone
```

A dense line may reduce to a sparse structural skeleton.

## Promotion decision

Promoted as a central generic melody architecture principle.

---

# 10. Passing notes

## Direct source observation

A skip between important tones may be filled by intermediate scale tones, and later chapters extend the process to:

- larger intervals;
- accented passing notes;
- chromatic passing notes;
- repeated passing notes;
- longer connective runs.

## Reusable invariant

Passing motion is a **connective surface function**:

```text
structural target A
-> connector
-> structural target B
```

This is directly relevant to repairing melodies that sound like isolated note dots.

## Promotion decision

Promoted into the generic Skill as an embellishment function.

No active Material card was created yet because density, accent and chromatic behavior should be validated in modern source / project contexts before style-specific promotion.

---

# 11. Neighboring notes

## Direct source observation

The source distinguishes neighboring notes from passing notes:

```text
neighbor:
principal -> adjacent tone -> same principal

passing:
principal A -> intermediate tone -> principal B
```

It then expands neighbor groups by combining them with chord tones, passing tones, both upper and lower neighbors and repetition.

## Reusable invariant

Neighbor motion is a **local ornament function**, while passing motion is a **progressive connector function**.

Complex surface groups can grow recursively from a small structural center.

## Promotion decision

Promoted into the generic Skill as a surface function, not a fixed ornamental rhythm.

---

# 12. Suspension and anticipation

## Direct source observation

The source treats Suspension as carrying an earlier tone into the temporal position of the following target before resolving.

Anticipation brings a future important tone forward into the preceding beat, usually briefly, before the structural occurrence.

The book notes that similar surface note patterns can admit different interpretations depending on harmonic conception.

## Reusable invariant

These are useful as **timing transformations around structural targets**:

```text
suspension:
old tone survives too long

anticipation:
new target arrives early
```

## Promotion decision

Promoted to the generic Skill as functional descriptions.

Exact common-practice notation and placement rules were not promoted.

---

# 13. Appoggiatura-family behavior

## Direct source observation

The book develops neighboring-note approaches into appoggiatura, double-appoggiatura and related groups, often using a tension tone before a principal structural tone.

## Reusable invariant

A reusable abstract function is:

```text
approach important target through local tension
-> establish target
```

## Promotion decision

Promoted at the functional level only.

No universal accent, duration, chromatic interval or density rule was promoted.

---

# 14. Evolution of melodic germs

## Direct source observation

Chapter XXXII explicitly describes complete ornate melodic figures or phrases evolving from two or three fundamental tones through embellishment and repetition.

The illustrated germs may be extremely simple scale-line or chord-line fragments.

The book requires the germ to be natural and the developed result to remain coherent, smooth and balanced.

## Reusable invariant

This is the strongest architecture result of the study:

```text
simple structural germ
-> manipulation
-> recurrence
-> embellishment
-> complete phrase
```

A melody system does not need to invent maximum novelty at every note.

## Promotion decision

Promoted as `germ-first reasoning` in the generic Skill.

---

# 15. Originality lives in manipulation

## Direct source observation

The source explicitly argues that simple germs themselves show little originality; individuality appears in the method and degree of their manipulation, embellishment and development.

## Reusable invariant

For an Agent:

```text
simple reusable seed
+
rich transformation system
=
large creative space
```

This supports investing in development operators rather than merely expanding a database of finished melodies.

---

# 16. Embellishment can disguise recurrence

## Direct source observation

Chapter XXXIII shows that embellishment may disguise or strengthen repetitions, sequences and recurrences of earlier figures or phrases.

## Reusable invariant

Analysis should distinguish:

```text
surface similarity
structural similarity
```

Two phrases may look very different note-by-note while remaining structurally derived from the same material.

## Future validation target

This is a strong candidate for MIDI-analysis tooling:

```text
surface melody
-> structural reduction
-> motif / germ matching
```

No automated implementation is claimed yet.

---

# 17. Expression and style

## Direct source observation

The conclusion explicitly separates systematic melody invention from later expression / conception and says conventional styles are best learned through observation.

## Reusable invariant

```text
melody grammar
!= style
!= instrument realization
```

Generic structure should be combined with style knowledge learned from relevant Materials / source studies.

## Promotion decision

Promoted as an architectural boundary.

The source's period-specific emotional characterizations of major/minor, duple/triple and tempo are not treated as universal style rules.

---

# 18. Vocal setting

## Direct source observation

The final section uses text setting to connect melodic rhythm, stress and emotional emphasis with language.

Important / accented words are generally given stronger musical prominence in the source's pedagogical model.

## Reusable invariant

Vocal melody should condition generic melodic planning on:

```text
syllables
stress
word importance
phrase punctuation
emotion
breath / melisma constraints
```

## Historical / style boundary

The exact mapping from lexical stress to higher / longer / metrically stronger notes is a useful default tendency, not a universal rule for modern vocal styles.

# Promotion summary

## Promoted to active Skill

Created:

```text
skills_v2/melody_structure_development/SKILL.md
```

Promoted concepts:

- structural versus surface tone hierarchy;
- germ-first composition;
- motif identity;
- recurrence before unrelated regeneration;
- modified / partial recurrence;
- phrase relation as a similarity continuum;
- rests as syntax;
- passing / neighbor / anticipation / suspension / appoggiatura as surface functions;
- harmonic relationship focused on deliberate arrivals rather than chord-tone prison;
- hierarchical critic reasoning;
- separation of generic melody, style and instrument realization.

## Not promoted as universal rules

- fixed four-bar phrases;
- fixed eight-bar Periods;
- fixed sixteen-bar Double-periods;
- mandatory tonic cadence;
- rigid active-scale-degree resolutions;
- universal avoidance of augmented intervals;
- related-key-only modulation;
- `irregular rhythm = bad`;
- historical claims about the emotional character of every mode, meter or tempo.

## Materials deliberately deferred

No book-derived Material card is activated from this study alone.

Candidate future cards should be promoted only after modern MIDI / score study or listening validation, for example:

```text
structural-target-with-passing-connectors
neighbor-based-local-ornament
motif-recurrence-with-controlled-variation
phrase-answer-development
```

The source provides theory evidence that these devices exist and are structurally useful. It does not by itself establish style-specific density, timing or instrumentation behavior for the current project.

# Recommended empirical follow-up

Use real human MIDI / score sources to test whether the generic abstractions survive modern practice.

For each melody-bearing source, attempt to infer:

```text
1. structural targets
2. candidate germ / motif
3. recurrence map
4. transformation type
5. phrase relation
6. surface embellishment roles
7. climax / register trajectory
8. instrument-specific realization
```

Then promote only the smallest behavior that remains useful after listening or source validation.
