# Gentle Steel Strum-Picking

## Identity

A laid-back, soft steel-string acoustic accompaniment that sits between light strumming and picking. It is designed to remain present for long stretches without sounding busy or rigid.

This is its own accompaniment family. It is not a softened version of `rolling-triplet-acoustic-strum`.

Useful tags:

```text
gentle
laid-back
steel-string
strum-picking
triplet-derived
persistent
ringing
vocal-support
```

## What makes it distinct

Compared with a rolling western-style triplet strum:

```text
rolling-triplet-acoustic-strum
= clear beat anchor + late-triplet return
= stronger repeated D . U identity
= broader directional sweep motion

gentle-steel-strum-picking
= mixed single notes + small partial strums
= softer and less insistently directional
= triplet-derived timing is a framework, not a fixed D . U formula
= long-running, low-variation accompaniment bed
```

Do not force this family into a beat-by-beat down/up sweep cycle.

## Core principle

Prefer:

```text
stable accompaniment identity
>
constant bar-to-bar invention
```

Once the guitar finds a comfortable motion, let it continue across many bars. Harmony may change while the accompaniment vocabulary remains recognizably the same.

## Event vocabulary

Use a restrained mixture of:

- single bass notes;
- single inner- or upper-string notes;
- two- or three-note partial strums;
- occasional broader arrival strokes;
- ringing common tones;
- deliberate gaps under important foreground phrases.

The part should naturally blur the line between picking and strumming.

Do not turn every event into a full chord block.

## Timing feel

Triplet-derived spacing is useful, especially relationships near:

```text
1/3 beat
2/3 beat
1 beat
```

But these are local timing relationships, not a requirement to sound every triplet position.

The result should feel gently rocking and continuous rather than shuffled aggressively.

## Density and variation

Keep the guitar active for long sections, but vary only a small amount at a time:

- omit one small attack;
- replace a partial strum with a single note;
- broaden an arrival;
- shift emphasis between bass and upper voices;
- create extra space under a vocal line;
- return to the familiar pattern afterward.

Large rhythmic transformations belong at genuine section changes.

## Dynamics

Keep the performance restrained.

Useful relationships:

- bass anchors may be slightly firmer;
- upper answers may be lighter;
- partial strums should remain softer than foreground rock strumming;
- increase width and density before making large velocity jumps.

## Sustain

Let compatible notes ring through later attacks.

A useful relationship is:

```text
soft pulse
+
ringing harmony
+
small re-articulations
```

Hard-gating every note will destroy the relaxed character.

## Arrangement role

This material works well as a nearly continuous guitar bed beneath vocals or a lead line.

The guitar should usually feel like a stable surface rather than a sequence of attention-seeking gestures.

## Failure modes

Revise when:

- the part turns into a rigid `D . U` western shuffle;
- every bar introduces a new pattern;
- every attack becomes a full chord;
- the guitar becomes a foreground melody;
- all notes are clipped short;
- the part becomes so sparse that it stops functioning as a continuous accompaniment layer;
- triplet-derived timing is flattened without intention.

## Study provenance

This material was abstracted from a user-provided MIDI study of a gentle steel-string pop accompaniment.

Observed in the studied MIDI:

- tempo was about 111 BPM;
- two long-running acoustic-guitar channels used GM program 25, Acoustic Guitar (steel);
- one channel contained 490 note-ons across roughly beats 8-461 and had median velocity around 72;
- another contained 896 note-ons across roughly beats 10-460 and had median velocity around 55;
- after grouping close note-ons, one guitar was dominated by single-note attacks while the other mixed many single notes with many three-note partial groups;
- common adjacent attack gaps were approximately one-third, two-thirds and one beat.

These observations support an independent gentle strum-picking accompaniment family. The source's exact pitches, harmony and full rhythmic sequence are intentionally omitted.
