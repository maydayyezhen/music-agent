# Persistent Gentle Steel-String Accompaniment

## Identity

A soft steel-string acoustic accompaniment that remains present for long stretches with a stable playing identity. The part should support the song continuously without demanding large bar-to-bar novelty.

Useful tags:

```text
gentle
laid-back
steel-string
persistent
triplet-derived
strum-picking
vocal-support
low-variation
```

The important abstraction is not whether every event is technically a strum or a picked note. The useful behavior is a persistent accompaniment bed made from a stable mixture of single-note picking, small partial strums and ringing harmony.

## Core principle

Prefer:

```text
pattern identity > bar-to-bar novelty
```

Once a comfortable accompaniment motion is established, let it continue across many bars. Change the harmony underneath it and introduce only small local variations.

Do not redesign the guitar part every bar merely to make it look more sophisticated.

## Event vocabulary

Build the texture from a restrained mixture of:

- single bass or inner-string notes;
- one-note upper-string answers;
- two- or three-note partial strums;
- occasional broader arrival strokes;
- ringing common tones;
- deliberate holes where the vocal or phrase needs space.

The part may sit on the boundary between picking and strumming. Keep that boundary rather than forcing every event into one category.

## Timing feel

A triplet-derived or long-short lattice works well for this family. Common local spacing may alternate between roughly one-third and two-thirds of a beat, with occasional full-beat gaps.

Use the timing as a soft rocking framework, not as a requirement to fill every triplet slot.

The groove should feel continuous even when the audible attacks are sparse.

## Density and variation

Keep the accompaniment active across long sections, but vary only one or two dimensions at a time:

- omit one small return attack;
- replace a three-note partial with a single note;
- broaden an arrival stroke;
- move emphasis from bass to upper strings;
- leave a little more space under a vocal phrase;
- restore the familiar pattern after the phrase.

Large rhythmic redesigns should be reserved for real section changes.

## Dynamics

Use a restrained velocity range appropriate to a supporting acoustic layer.

Useful relationships:

- bass anchors may be slightly firmer;
- small upper-string answers can be lighter;
- partial strums should remain softer than a foreground rock strum;
- section growth should come gradually through width and density before large velocity jumps.

## Sustain

Let compatible notes ring. This style loses its relaxed character when every event is hard-gated at the next attack.

A useful relationship is:

```text
stable pulse
+
ringing harmony
+
small local re-articulation
```

## Arrangement role

This material is especially useful when the guitar acts as a nearly continuous bed under a vocal or lead line.

The guitar should usually be felt as a stable surface rather than heard as a sequence of independent gestures.

Use stronger transformations only at section boundaries, fills or arrangement lifts.

## Failure modes

Revise when:

- every bar introduces a new accompaniment idea;
- the guitar becomes a foreground melody instead of a support layer;
- every attack is a full block chord;
- all notes are clipped short;
- the triplet-derived feel is flattened into rigid equal spacing without intention;
- the part becomes so sparse that it stops functioning as a persistent bed;
- variation is random rather than tied to phrase and section role.

## Study provenance

This material was abstracted from a user-provided MIDI study of a gentle steel-string pop accompaniment.

Observed in the studied MIDI:

- tempo was about 111 BPM;
- two long-running acoustic-guitar channels used GM program 25, Acoustic Guitar (steel);
- one steel-string channel ran from roughly beat 8 to beat 461 and contained 490 note-ons;
- another ran from roughly beat 10 to beat 460 and contained 896 note-ons;
- after grouping very close note-ons, one channel was dominated by single-note attacks while the other mixed many single notes with many three-note partial groups;
- common adjacent attack gaps were approximately one-third, two-thirds and one beat;
- median note-on velocity was about 72 on one guitar channel and about 55 on the lighter companion channel.

These observations support a persistent, low-variation steel-string accompaniment bed that mixes picking and small strums. The source's exact pitches, harmony, full rhythmic sequence and arrangement are intentionally omitted.
