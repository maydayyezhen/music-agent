# Instrument Notes

## Piano — Acoustic Grand Piano (GM program 0)

Carries the recognizable motif and section melody. Left hand is limited to occasional roots/fifths or two-note shells; right hand uses melody and small color-tone dyads. It must breathe, especially in Intro and Return, and must not become continuous chord carpet.

## Bass — Electric Bass (finger) (GM program 33)

Enters in A. Uses roots, fifths, thirds, stepwise approaches, and rests. Important attacks support the kick, while pickups on beat 4 or 4.5 lead into the following harmony. Register stays mainly D2–C#3.

## Guitar — Electric Guitar (clean) (GM program 27)

Song-local override of the shared overdriven-guitar mapping. Uses playable upper-string dyads and broken triads around D3–E5, never duplicates the piano rhythm. A section is sparse arpeggio; B adds off-beat two-note responses; Outro uses a long, bell-like D–A color.

## Strings — String Ensemble 1 (GM program 48)

Absent from Intro and A. Enters in B with sustained, voice-led upper support and a small contrary counter-line. It does not copy piano blocks. It leaves before the final two bars so the close feels personal again.

## Pad — Pad 2 (warm) (GM program 89)

Very low-velocity, spread voicings above the bass mud zone. Provides dusk atmosphere and harmonic glue, disappearing enough in Return for the last re-entry to register.

## Drums — Standard Drum Kit (GM channel 10)

Light pop ballad groove: sparse kick, side-stick in A, regular snare only in B, gently accented eighth-note hats, and restrained fills at structural boundaries. Main anchors remain tight; variation comes from hat velocity and selective open hats rather than strong timing randomness.
