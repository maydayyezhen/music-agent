# Instrument Notes

The song uses only the verified mappings in the local GeneralUser GS SoundFont.

## Piano — Acoustic Grand Piano (GM program 0)

- Intro: broken Dadd9-family voicings and the first motif statement.
- Verse: light right-hand syncopation; no thick low block chords.
- Pre: ascending eighth-note arpeggios that outline each chord and increase expectation.
- Chorus: hook carrier plus selective upper-register chord punctuation.
- Outro: reduces to a clear Dadd9 cadence and small afterglow.

## Bass — Electric Bass (finger) (GM program 33)

- Walking-forward eighth-note line built from roots, fifths, thirds, passing tones and semitone/step approaches.
- Locks major attacks with kick but answers independently on offbeats.
- Uses C# under A and F# under D where the harmony calls for inversions; avoids unbroken root transcription.

## Guitar — Overdriven Guitar (GM program 29)

- Intro: short octave/power-shape answers.
- Verse: playable muted fifth-based pulses, leaving gaps around the melody.
- Pre: longer power chords with rising roots.
- Chorus: low power-chord drive on eighth-note strums plus high octave responses; intentionally unlike the piano's hook and upper voicings.
- Final four bars: exits completely to reveal the emotional landing.

## Strings — String Ensemble 1 (GM program 48)

- Enter in Pre-Chorus with singable stepwise ascent.
- Chorus: sustained upper support and a restrained counterline, not copied piano chords.
- Outro: descending inner line and final held D/A/F# color.

## Pad — Pad 2 (warm) (GM program 89)

- Broad voicings above the bass register, low velocity, slow attacks.
- Withheld in the first half of Verse; returns through Pre and Chorus for width.
- Sustains the final Dadd9 color beneath the piano release.

## Drums — Standard Drum Kit (GM channel 10)

- Verse: tight pop-rock kick/snare with accented eighth hats.
- Pre: increasing density, open-hat lift, and a final snare/tom fill.
- Chorus: crash-confirmed arrivals, busier kick, selective open hats; boundary fills at bars 28 and 36.
- Anchors stay on-grid; hat velocities and secondary hits provide restrained human variation.
